\begin{abstract}
We map the fine-tuning feasibility boundary of a 16\,GiB Apple~M4 Mac mini
with a preregistered benchmark: 118 supervised runs over the Qwen3 family
(0.6B--14B), BF16 LoRA vs.\ 4-bit QLoRA, context lengths 512--8192, LoRA
ranks 4--32, and micro-batches 1--8, each with full provenance (git commit,
model revision, per-second swap sampling, SHA-256 manifests). We find:
(1) 4-bit QLoRA cuts peak training memory to 0.54--0.73$\times$ of BF16,
and 8B is the highest scale whose preregistered seed set completed
three-of-three within one favorable residency window; step-time ratios
of 1.10--1.23 stay inside the preregistered $\pm$25\% equivalence margin
at both fully paired scales (the third scale contributes a single
cross-window pair, reported as such). (2) The transition from
reproducibly trainable configurations to system-state-dependent boundary
behavior is directly observable: the same 4B BF16 or 14B 4-bit
configuration that trains at zero or low swap residency stalls at 70\%
completion or makes zero progress when several GiB of swap are resident.
We report 14B as an explicit boundary case rather than a completed
formal cell. (3) Peak memory grows \emph{sub}linearly with model size
over the measured range (log-log slope 0.57 for 4-bit, 95\% CI
$[0.37,0.78]$), consistent with fixed-overhead dilution, while step time
grows approximately linearly (slope 1.00; absolute step times are
window-specific and only within-window ratios are compared). (4) Context
length, not model scale, is the binding constraint: 2048-token contexts
complete but push the allocator high-water mark to 23.9\,GiB (beyond
physical RAM, carried by macOS paging), and 4096-token contexts are
killed before their first step. (5) Micro-batch scaling fails outright
at batch~8 (external SIGKILL, with a kernel jetsam record confirming
one kill, while system-wide swap usage nears 20\,GiB) and \emph{reduces}
throughput at every trainable size. Every number in this paper is
generated programmatically from immutable raw results; nine
preregistration deviations, including one post-result audit-rule
revision, are reported in full.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Consumer Apple Silicon machines ship with a single 16\,GiB unified-memory
pool that the GPU shares with the OS. Whether, and under what conditions,
such a machine can fine-tune modern LLMs is usually answered anecdotally.
This paper answers it with a preregistered, failure-inclusive benchmark:
every configuration in a frozen matrix was run under an experiment
supervisor that recorded provenance, per-second swap sampling, and
complete logs. Every completed or failed run is retained as evidence.

Our contributions: (i) a preregistered feasibility map for Qwen3
0.6B--14B under BF16 LoRA, 4-bit QLoRA, and (exploratory) full
fine-tuning on a single M4/16\,GiB machine, with 8B 4-bit identified
as the highest configuration whose preregistered seed set completed
three-of-three in one favorable residency window and 14B reported as an
explicit system-state-dependent boundary case; (ii) quantified
memory/time trade-offs with frozen equivalence margins; (iii)
context-length and micro-batch boundaries expressed as intervals with
system-state confounders documented; (iv) an audit showing that
\trainable is a joint property of the model and the machine's
memory-residency state, not of the model alone; and (v) a full
reproducibility chain from raw immutable results to every number in
this paper, including nine logged deviations from preregistration.

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\subsection{Apple Silicon and Unified Memory}
Apple Silicon system-on-chips (SoCs) present a single physical memory pool shared by CPU and GPU.
\texttt{mlx}~\cite{mlx2023} is an array framework that targets this architecture
directly; \texttt{mlx-lm}~\cite{mlxlm} provides LLM fine-tuning on top of it.
Prior profiling of Apple Silicon training reports substantial gaps to NVIDIA
GPUs and identifies page faults and kernel-launch overhead as system-level
contributors~\cite{feng2025profiling}. Formal MLX benchmarking has centered
on inference latency for encoder models~\cite{ajayi2025benchmarking};
training-side MLX reports exist as application case
studies~\cite{baral2026distillation}; consumer-device LLM stacks beyond MLX
include llama.cpp~\cite{llamacpp}, and a recent comparative study benchmarks
five local inference runtimes on Apple Silicon~\cite{rajesh2025production}.

\subsection{MLX}
The MLX framework exposes Metal-backed computation with lazy evaluation and a
composable optimizer API~\cite{mlx2023}. Our measurement validation
(Sec.~\ref{sec:measurements}) quantifies why explicit synchronization is
required for per-step timing under lazy execution.

\subsection{LoRA and QLoRA}
LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora} parameterizes weight updates with low-rank adapters;
QLoRA~\cite{dettmers2023qlora} backpropagates through 4-bit quantized frozen
weights, following the broader line of LLM quantization
work~\cite{dettmers2022llmint8}. Both reduce the memory footprint relative to
full fine-tuning~\cite{houlsby2019parameter,chen2016training};
GaLore~\cite{zhao2024galore} targets the same budget from the optimizer-state
side, LOMO~\cite{lv2024lomo} from fused low-memory updates, and
general-purpose fine-tuning frameworks such as
LlamaFactory~\cite{zheng2024llamafactory} unify these methods behind one
interface. QLoRA's ``paged optimizers'' manage GPU memory spikes with
CUDA unified-memory paging---a framework-controlled mechanism that has no
equivalent in our setting, where macOS owns paging globally and the training
process cannot control it (Sec.~\ref{sec:qlorasem}). These methods are
largely evaluated on data-center GPUs; how they behave inside a 16\,GB
unified-memory budget is the subject of this paper. On-device fine-tuning
on phones and similarly constrained hardware is an adjacent line of
work~\cite{geng2025mobilefinetuner}, focused on framework engineering
rather than feasibility boundaries.

\subsection{Resource-Constrained LLM Fine-Tuning}
Resource-constrained fine-tuning has been studied mainly on GPUs and
mobile devices: memory-efficiency methods such as
GaLore~\cite{zhao2024galore} and QLoRA~\cite{dettmers2023qlora} report
feasibility on GPU hardware where CPU and GPU memory are separate, and
on-device frameworks~\cite{geng2025mobilefinetuner} engineer around
phone memory budgets; application studies do report Apple Silicon + MLX
LoRA runs~\cite{baral2026distillation} but without characterizing the
feasibility boundary. To our knowledge, that boundary on a fixed
consumer unified-memory budget has not been mapped by a preregistered
benchmark: which model scales, precisions, and context lengths admit
\emph{completed} training steps, and at what cost? We found no
published quantitative boundary for this class of hardware to compare
against---the closest works above report feasibility impressions or
application outcomes, not boundary measurements---so this paper
establishes the reference numbers rather than refining existing ones.
Unified memory changes the failure mode relative to GPUs (page
thrashing rather than allocator exceptions), which motivates our
measurement design.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Research Questions}
We study:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RQ1} How do memory, step time, and throughput of BF16 LoRA and
        4-bit QLoRA scale with model size?
  \item \textbf{RQ2} How far does 4-bit quantization extend the
        \trainable boundary?
  \item \textbf{RQ3} Where is the context-length boundary?
  \item \textbf{RQ4} Does the memory saving of quantization come with a
        measurable training-time penalty?
  \item \textbf{RQ5} How large is the gap between ``training can start'' and
        \practical fine-tuning?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Hardware and Software}
All experiments run on a single Apple Mac mini with an Apple M4 SoC, 16\,GiB
unified memory, and macOS~26.5; the exact preflight-recorded environment is in
Table~\ref{tab:setup}. Software was locked with uv: Python~3.13.11,
\texttt{mlx}~0.32.2, \texttt{mlx-lm}~0.31.3. The M4 generation context is from
Apple's release material~\cite{applem4}.

\subsection{Models}
We use the Qwen3 family~\cite{yang2025qwen3}: instruction-tuned chat models
(not base models) at 0.6B/1.7B/4B in BF16 and 0.6B--14B as pre-quantized 4-bit
mlx-community conversions; every repository is pinned to a Hub revision and
byte-verified (Table~\ref{tab:setup}).

\subsection{Dataset}
The formal benchmark uses a frozen subset of
ultrachat\_200k~\cite{ding2023ultrachat} (revision \texttt{8049631c}): 2{,}048
training and 32 validation conversations sampled with seed 42 and formatted
with the Qwen3 chat template; loss is a standard language-model (LM) loss over all tokens.
The manifest and SHA-256 sums ship with the repository. Synthetic long-context
workloads are used only for context-scaling systems analysis, never for
quality claims.

\subsection{Fine-Tuning Methods}
\label{sec:qlorasem}
BF16 LoRA (rank 8, $\alpha$=16, dropout 0, targets q/k/v/o\_proj), 4-bit QLoRA
(same adapters, group size 64), and, for the smallest model only, full
fine-tuning. Optimizer AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2019adamw}, lr $10^{-4}$,
constant schedule, micro-batch 1.

\paragraph{QLoRA semantics on MLX}
Our ``4-bit QLoRA'' is the \texttt{mlx-lm} path: LoRA adapters over a
\emph{pre-quantized} 4-bit base with group-wise affine quantization
(group size 64), trained by regular AdamW. It differs from the original
QLoRA recipe~\cite{dettmers2023qlora} in three ways: the 4-bit format is
group-wise affine, not NF4; there is no double quantization of the
quantization constants; and there is no paged optimizer---on macOS the
OS owns paging globally, so the memory-spike management that QLoRA
performs in-framework on GPUs is neither available nor needed in the
same form. Numbers in this paper are therefore comparable to the QLoRA
\emph{line} of work (low-rank adapters over frozen quantized weights)
but not configuration-identical to Dettmers et al.'s setup.

\subsection{Measurement Methodology}
\label{sec:measurements}
Every run is launched by an Experiment Supervisor
(Figure~\ref{fig:arch}) that records git provenance,
hardware, memory/swap state (before, after, and 1\,Hz sampling), full
stdout/stderr, per-step timings, and an atomic SHA-256 manifest; failures are
first-class results. Step timing includes forward, backward, optimizer update,
and an explicit \texttt{mx.eval} synchronization; we measured that omitting
this synchronization under-reports step time by three orders of magnitude
under MLX's lazy execution. Peak memory is the MLX allocator's Metal-buffer
high-water mark (\texttt{get\_peak\_memory}); it is not process resident set size (RSS), and on
unified memory it may exceed physical RAM, at which point macOS paging carries
the excess. The before/after snapshots additionally record the full
\texttt{vm\_stat} counter set (swap-ins/outs, compressor occupancy,
compressions/decompressions); the 1\,Hz trajectory samples system-wide swap
only. Paging intensity per run is computed from the \texttt{vm\_stat}
snapshots as swap-in bytes per completed step (the D4 stratification of
Table~\ref{tab:tier}). System-level counters include background processes,
sampled peaks may miss transients between samples, and the reported peak is
the maximum \emph{observed} value---the validation details and these
limitations are in the repository
(\texttt{docs/measurement\_validation.md}).

\subsection{Operational Definitions}
\label{sec:definitions}
\loadable: the training process loads the model weights.
\trainable: the run completes every requested optimizer step with
\texttt{terminal\_state=success} and no kill signal.
\practical: \trainable and (P1) sampled peak system swap does not exceed the
pre-run level by more than 4\,GiB, and (P2) median step time $\le$10\,s.
\efficient: \practical with throughput $\ge$100 loss-bearing tokens/s.
All thresholds were frozen before any formal run (commit \texttt{3dc33d2},
2026-09-12; \texttt{research/preregistration.md}); sensitivity to $\pm
2\times$ threshold changes is reported in Table~\ref{tab:sens}, where the
system-wide P1 criterion is shown to be confounded by background-process
swap noise (Sec.~\ref{sec:whatenable}). SIGKILL terminations are never
reclassified as OOM without direct kernel evidence.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
Formal configurations, the dataset freeze, the analysis pipeline, and this
paper's tables and figures are generated from raw results by committed scripts;
no number is hand-typed. Appendix~\ref{app:repro} lists reproduction commands.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Model-Scale Feasibility}
Table~\ref{tab:matrix} and Figure~\ref{fig:feas} summarize the
preregistered matrix at ctx512.
All BF16 LoRA and 4-bit QLoRA cells through 1.7B complete their three
seeds, and 4-bit completes at 4B and 8B as well (8B: three seeds,
1{,}643--1{,}693\,s per 100-step run, 9.19\,GiB peak). The three 8B
successes all fall in one 3.6--4.8\,GiB-residency window on 2026-09-15;
an earlier first attempt at 16.4\,GiB residency timed out and is
retained as system-state evidence (D7)---the same regime dependence the
boundary cells exhibit, read here in the favorable direction.
Three-of-three seeds also implies a wide success-rate interval
(Clopper--Pearson 95\% CI lower bound 0.29), so we read
``reproducibly trainable'' as \emph{repeatable within one observed
window}. 8B 4-bit is thus the highest configuration with a completed
preregistered seed set in this benchmark. The
14B 4-bit cell is deliberately reported as a system-state-dependent boundary
case rather than a completed or failed formal cell (deviation D8;
Sec.~\ref{sec:boundary14b}). The 4B BF16 cell shows regime dependence
(D1): it completed in 451\,s at zero residency but produced zero completed
steps in 27 minutes at 6.1--6.3\,GiB residency. 8B BF16 was preregistered as a
probe only and timed out; 14B BF16 was declared out-of-budget without
observation. Full fine-tuning of 0.6B completes but diverges at the
frozen lr $10^{-4}$ (final validation loss 4.44 vs.\ 1.93 for 4-bit
QLoRA), a negative quality result we retain as-is.

\subsection{Memory Scaling}
Figure~\ref{fig:memscale} shows peak MLX-allocator memory versus logical
parameters. Memory grows \emph{sub}linearly over the measured range:
log-log slope $0.57\pm0.05$ (95\% CI $[0.37,0.78]$, $n$=4) for 4-bit
QLoRA, an interval that excludes the parameter-proportional value of 1.
The BF16 series has three points; its slope $0.71\pm0.07$ (CI
$[-0.22,1.65]$) carries no inferential weight at that sample size. A
power law and the affine model $\mathrm{peak}\approx w\!\cdot\!\mathrm{params}+c$
fit the 4-bit points comparably (raw-space $\Delta$AIC 3.2 in favor of
the power law, both $R^2>0.98$), and the affine fit quantifies the
mechanism directly: a fixed non-weight component of $\sim$1.8\,GiB
(intercept) plus $\sim$0.93\,GiB per B parameters. Fixed-overhead
dilution is visible in the split: at 0.6B the 4-bit weights occupy
only $\sim$0.31\,GiB of a 2.13\,GiB peak, while at 8B the split is
4.29\,GiB weights inside a 9.19\,GiB peak. We report audit H4
(approximate linearity) as unsupported over this range, with an
explicit caveat: sub-linearity is a bounded-range observation. The
affine mechanism approaches slope 1 as parameters grow, and the 8B
allocator peak of 9.19\,GiB says nothing about 14B without new
measurements. Within this range, memory ceilings bind later than
parameter-proportional estimates suggest.

\subsection{Training-Time Scaling}
\label{sec:timingscale}
Step time scales approximately linearly, not sublinearly, with model
size (Figure~\ref{fig:timescale}; log-log slope $1.00\pm0.09$, 95\% CI
$[0.62,1.37]$, $R^2$=0.985, 4-bit series, $n$=4; audit H5: partially
supported, approximately-linear band). Under the preregistered paging
stratification (D4) the picture is mixed rather than uniformly
paging-confounded (Table~\ref{tab:tier}): the 0.6B/1.7B 4-bit groups
and the 0.6B BF16 group are Tier-A ($<$50\,MB swap-in per step), while
the 4B and 8B groups are Tier-B (100--122 and 732--848\,MB/step
respectively). An earlier draft, and the audit behind it, labeled every
run Tier-B; that label traced to an analysis-script defect (the paging
metric silently failed on the flattened results table and returned
infinity for all runs) and is corrected here as deviation D9. Absolute
step times remain descriptive, window-specific values: the same 4B
4-bit b1 configuration measured 3.66\,s/step in the 100-step axis-1
window (6.3--7.9\,GiB residency, 100--122\,MB/step) and 5.62\,s/step in
the 20-step batch-axis window (2.9--3.2\,GiB residency, 231--339\,MB/step;
Table~\ref{tab:batch})---a 53\% window drift that exceeds the $\pm$25\%
equivalence margin, and that we therefore treat as window state, not
signal. Cross-model \emph{ratios} and paired comparisons within one
window remain interpretable; absolute latencies from different windows
are not compared in this paper.

\subsection{Quantization Trade-offs}
Across the paired scales (Figure~\ref{fig:paired},
Table~\ref{tab:paired}), 4-bit QLoRA reduces peak memory to
0.73$\times$ (0.6B), 0.59$\times$ (1.7B), and 0.54$\times$ (4B) of the
BF16 LoRA value ($n$=7 seed-pairs; audit H1: supported). Step-time
ratios of 1.10--1.14 and 1.21--1.23 at 0.6B and 1.7B sit inside the
preregistered $\pm$25\% equivalence margin (audit H3: supported, ``no
practically meaningful timing penalty under the frozen margin''). The
4B ratio of 1.21 is a single seed pair whose BF16 denominator is the
zero-residency D1 rerun---a cross-window observation
(Sec.~\ref{sec:timingscale})---so equivalence at 4B is descriptive
rather than a within-window verdict, and we count only the two fully
paired scales in the abstract. Memory ratios are window-robust:
allocator peaks are bit-identical across seeds ($\pm$0.00 in
Table~\ref{tab:matrix}). Validation losses after 100 steps
\emph{differ} between precisions at both scales---0.6B: 1.93 vs.\ 1.81
(paired $t=70.4$, $p=2\times10^{-4}$, $n$=3 pairs); 1.7B: 1.63 vs.\
1.55 (paired $t=13.4$, $p=0.006$)---the 0.085--0.113 absolute
differences are many seed-SDs wide and the 95\% CIs do not overlap. The
difference is real, small in absolute terms, and points in the
direction expected of 4-bit quantization noise; it is \emph{not}
evidence of training-quality equivalence, which 100-step LM loss
cannot establish (Sec.~\ref{sec:effectiveness}).

\subsection{Batch and Rank Sensitivity}
Micro-batch scaling on 4-bit 4B (Table~\ref{tab:batch},
Figure~\ref{fig:batch}) monotonically \emph{reduces} throughput, from
89.5 loss-bearing tokens/s at batch~1 to 66.4 at batch~2 and 34.6 at
batch~4, while peak memory climbs 6.19$\rightarrow$10.04$\rightarrow$16.98\,GiB
and per-step swap-in grows from 231--339\,MB to 13.1--13.6\,GB.
Batch~8 is worse still: the process is killed by an external SIGKILL
before it completes a step in all three seeds (exit 137, zero stdout,
system-wide swap usage near 20\,GiB). For the last of the three
batch-8 kills the kernel's own jetsam record is retained
(Appendix~\ref{app:jetsam}): the training process held 23.5\,GiB
resident (lifetime maximum 29.7\,GiB) while the compressor carried
12.5\,GiB and 59\,MB of pages remained free---direct kernel evidence
that this kill was memory exhaustion. The remaining five SIGKILL runs
in the taxonomy have no retained jetsam record and are labeled by exit
evidence only. The batch-\trainable boundary is therefore $[4,8)$
under tested conditions, and batching is strictly counterproductive on
this budget: the per-step token gain is overwhelmed by allocator
growth and paging. All batch-axis comparisons are within one window;
the 3.66\,s axis-1 value for the same b1 configuration reflects a
different window (Sec.~\ref{sec:timingscale}). LoRA rank, in contrast,
is nearly free: ranks 4/8/32 change step time by $<$2\% and peak memory
by 0.24\,GiB (Figure~\ref{fig:rank}, Table~\ref{tab:rank}).

\subsection{Context-Length Boundary}
Context length dominates every other axis (Figure~\ref{fig:ctx}): at 4B
4-bit, ctx1024 multiplies median step time by 6.5$\times$ versus ctx512
(3.66$\rightarrow$24.0\,s) and peak memory by 1.8$\times$
(6.19$\rightarrow$11.16\,GiB); ctx2048 completed all three
preregistered seeds---two in the 2026-09-12 window (6.5--7.9\,GiB
residency) and one rerun of s123 at 2.96\,GiB residency after its first
attempt was SIGKILLed at 7.66\,GiB (the failed attempt is the ctx2048
entry in Table~\ref{tab:failures}); the allocator high-water mark
reached 23.9\,GiB, far beyond physical RAM, carried by macOS paging
(system-wide swap usage peaked near 20\,GiB during these runs). At
ctx4096 and ctx8192 the process is killed by an external SIGKILL
before completing a single step. The \trainable context boundary is
thus $[2048,4096)$, reported as an interval with two evidence-grade
caveats: the lower edge rests on completed formal seeds, while the
upper edge rests on single-seed SIGKILL probes under a synthetic
long-context workload, so the interval states where failure was
observed, not a measured threshold. Within Tier-B formal data the time
amplification over ctx512 (5.6$\times$ at 2048) only modestly exceeds
the 4$\times$ token-count increase, so we report the boundary but not a
dramatic time ``cliff'' (audit H6: partially supported, v2 rule; the
preregistered-era v1 rule and its revision are disclosed as D9,
App.~\ref{app:deviations}).

\subsection{Training Effectiveness}
\label{sec:effectiveness}
Validation loss decreases monotonically along the recorded trajectory
in every trainable run, and the precision difference after 100 steps is
small in absolute terms (0.085--0.113) though statistically clear
(Sec.~Quantization Trade-offs). The divergent full-FT cell (0.6B,
lr $10^{-4}$, validation loss 4.44) is retained as a negative result. We
make no instruction-following or converged-quality claims: the objective
is LM loss on ultrachat\_200k with all tokens counted over 100 steps,
which supports feasibility and cost comparisons but not
training-quality equivalence between precisions.

\subsection{Boundary Behavior at 14B}
\label{sec:boundary14b}
We report the 14B 4-bit cell as an explicit boundary case study (D8):
a configuration observed to enter training under favorable conditions,
but not reproducibly sustained under the formal workload.

\paragraph{Observation}
Three raw records constitute the evidence. (1) A 20-step probe at
2.5\,GiB swap residency loaded and trained at 0.68\,s/step
(\texttt{success}). (2) A formal attempt launched at 6.6\,GiB residency
completed zero steps in 24 minutes before the operator interrupted it
(KeyboardInterrupt; recorded under an unresolved-failure terminal
state, D8). (3) A second formal attempt at 3.5\,GiB residency exhibited
$\sim$40 minutes of model loading, then progressed to 70 of 100 steps
(70 recorded step lines in the raw stdout log), with step
times of 9.4--105\,s that peaked mid-run and partially recovered,
before stalling for the final $\sim$23 minutes until the 7{,}200\,s
timeout killed it. Residency figures are the
pre-run \texttt{initial\_swap\_bytes} of each raw record. All three
records are retained unaltered; two further attempts were never
executed (D8).

\paragraph{Interpretation}
These observations are consistent with 14B crossing from reproducible
training into a regime where system memory residency and paging state
materially affect execution: the same regime dependence documented
for 4B BF16 (D1), reached earlier in the scale ladder. This is an
interpretation, not a direct measurement of untrainability: we did not
observe allocator exhaustion, and the probe demonstrates that favorable
conditions exist.

\paragraph{Limitation}
We did not complete a three-seed formal series at 14B, did not attempt
to manufacture a low-residency window (no reboots or system-state
manipulation to chase success, per D8), and therefore can neither claim
that 14B is reproducibly trainable nor rule it out on 16\,GiB; the
7{,}200\,s limit that terminated the second attempt is the
supervisor-wide configured bound (2{,}400--7{,}200\,s across formal
configs, Table~\ref{tab:failures}), not a per-cell tuned value, and its
sensitivity is untested. The
preregistered 14B ctx2048 probe was cancelled without execution. The
honest summary is: under the tested formal workload and observed system
states, 14B 4-bit QLoRA was not sustained to completion, while a
favorable-state probe did complete.

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{What Does 16\,GB Actually Enable?}
\label{sec:whatenable}
The results separate into three tiers once the frozen thresholds are
applied honestly (Table~\ref{tab:sens}). Through 4B, 4-bit QLoRA is
\trainable and \practical on P2 (median step $\le$10\,s) with stable
three-seed reproduction. At 8B, 4-bit remains \trainable but misses
the frozen P2 threshold by 0.039\,s (10.039 vs.\ 10\,s); under the
$\pm2\times$ sensitivity it is \practical{} at a 20\,s threshold and
fails at 5\,s, so we call it borderline-\practical{} rather than blur
the boundary. Its 42.9\,tokens/s also fails the \efficient{}
criterion, but that number is a Tier-B absolute value under the
resident paging state (Sec.~\ref{sec:timingscale}); a favorable window
could move it, which is why \efficient{} was preregistered as
exploratory. The P1 swap-growth criterion is system-wide and is
confounded by background load: measured swap growth was 5.2\,GiB on
the 0.6B cell but 3.1--3.7\,GiB on the 4B/8B cells (per-run peak-minus-
initial swap in the processed data; the resulting P1 pass/fail pattern
is in Table~\ref{tab:sens})---an inversion that tracks background
processes, not the training working set, so we do not gate \practical{}
on P1 alone. At 14B, behavior becomes
system-state-dependent: favorable conditions admit real training
steps, while the formal workload was not sustained under observed
residencies. BF16 LoRA is \practical through 1.7B. The binding
constraints are, in order, context length, micro-batch, then model
scale, inverting the usual intuition that parameters come first. The
most valuable single optimization for this class of hardware would
attack the fixed non-weight memory overhead that the affine memory fit
isolates ($\sim$1.8\,GiB intercept).

\subsection{Loadable vs Trainable vs Practical, and How to Report It}
The three-layer vocabulary is not academic: every ``boundary'' in this
benchmark moved with system state. The same 4B BF16 configuration is
\trainable in 451\,s at zero swap residency and cannot finish one step
in 27 minutes at 6.1--6.3\,GiB residency (D1); 14B 4-bit spans a
0.68\,s/step probe and a 70/100-step timeout (D7/D8); and 8B itself,
our ``reproducible'' boundary cell, timed out at 16.4\,GiB residency
before completing 3/3 in a 3.6--4.8\,GiB window (D7). The favorable
and unfavorable readings are the same kind of evidence, which is the
point. Feasibility claims that do not report swap residency are
therefore not reproducible in kind.

We recommend memory-residency state become a mandatory field in
consumer-hardware training reports. Concretely, the field should cover
(i) system-wide swap before/after and the 1\,Hz sampled peak, (ii) the
before/after \texttt{vm\_stat} snapshot including compressor occupancy
and swap-in/out counters (already recorded by our supervisor; per-step
paging intensity is derivable, Table~\ref{tab:tier}), and (iii)
per-process resident footprint where available. The analogy to clock
state in GPU benchmarks has a stated limit: clock state is a
device-local, standardized quantity, whereas system-wide swap is a
machine-history property shared with every other process, so residency
numbers are comparable across reports only alongside the sampling
protocol, not as bare values. The complementary route is
\emph{controlling} state---fresh-boot windows---which we deliberately
declined for the 14B cell (D8) to avoid a special-system-state
confounder; both routes belong in a reporting standard.

\subsection{Implications for Consumer LLM Research}
Three practical rules follow. (1) Quantization pays twice on unified
memory: it buys headroom (0.54--0.73$\times$ memory) at a time cost
measured at 1.10--1.23$\times$ and bounded inside the frozen $\pm$25\%
margin, and extends reach by at least two model sizes. (2) Do not
batch: on a paging-capable unified budget, throughput falls
monotonically with micro-batch and the hard failure at batch~8 arrives
abruptly (one kill kernel-verified, App.~\ref{app:jetsam}). (3) Treat
context as the primary resource: the [2048,4096) boundary with the
allocator peak at 23.9\,GiB (vs.\ 16\,GiB physical) shows the system
will carry the excess via swap until the process dies: ``it started''
says nothing about ``it will finish''.

\subsection{Limitations}
Single machine, single SoC generation (M4, 16\,GiB); all conclusions
are single-point observations on that machine. Absolute times are
window-specific under a mixed Tier-A/Tier-B picture (Table~\ref{tab:tier}),
so we compare only within-window ratios. The 8B ``reproducible'' cell
is three-of-three within one favorable window (first attempt at high
residency timed out, D7); its success-rate interval is wide
(Clopper--Pearson lower bound 0.29). Rerun decisions were not
uniformly preregistered: first attempts that failed for
environment-attributable reasons (8B s42, ctx2048 s123) were rerun
under the D7 information-gain rule with both outcomes retained, while
the 4B BF16 cell was frozen as boundary evidence (D1) and 14B attempts
were stopped (D8)---we state this asymmetry rather than smooth it
over. The 14B ``not sustained'' verdict is bounded by the 7{,}200\,s
supervisor timeout, whose sensitivity we did not measure. LM-loss-only
objectives with no prompt masking; 20-step runs on the
context/batch/rank axes limit distributional detail (54 measured steps
per cell after warm-up exclusion, 3 seeds $\times$ 18); system-level swap counters include
background processes; the context upper edge rests on single-seed
probes. Operational variables that matter to end users---thermal and
fan behavior under sustained GPU load, SSD write wear from multi-GiB
swap traffic, disk-space budgeting, and wall-clock extrapolation to
full training runs---are outside this benchmark's scope. Deviations
D1--D9 are summarized in
Appendix~\ref{app:deviations} and reported in full in the repository.

\subsection{Ethics and Licensing}
The benchmark targets publicly released model weights (Apache~2.0 for Qwen3)
and a publicly released dataset (ultrachat\_200k, MIT license);
no synthetic workload contributes to quality claims. All measurements were
performed on the authors' own hardware.

\section{Conclusion}
On this 16\,GiB unified-memory Mac, 4-bit QLoRA is the default answer
for fine-tuning: it roughly halves memory (0.54--0.73$\times$) at a
time cost of 1.10--1.23$\times$ that stays inside the frozen $\pm$25\%
margin, completes the preregistered seed set at 8B within one
favorable residency window, and leaves rank choice nearly free. The
boundaries that actually bind are context ($\in[2048,4096)$ tokens)
and micro-batch ($\in[4,8)$, with monotonically negative throughput
returns). Both boundaries, like the 4B BF16, 8B, and 14B cells, shift
with the machine's swap residency, making system-state reporting a
prerequisite for any reproducible consumer-hardware training claim.

scripts; the failures are part of the result.

\section{Kernel Jetsam Evidence for One Batch-8 Kill}

During revision we retrieved the kernel-generated jetsam report
\texttt{JetsamEvent-2026-09-16-004948.ips} (bug\_type 298) from
\texttt{/Library/Logs/DiagnosticReports/}; a copy with SHA-256
sidecar is preserved at \texttt{research/evidence/}. Its timestamp
(2026-09-16 00:49:48 local) falls inside the third batch-8 run's
execution window (started 00:45:51). The report names
\texttt{python3.13} (the training process) as the largest process
with 1{,}539{,}055 resident pages ($\approx$23.5\,GiB at 16\,KiB
pages; lifetime maximum $\approx$29.7\,GiB), while the memory
compressor held 12.5\,GiB and only 59\,MiB of pages remained free,
with 24 processes jettisoned for compressor-space shortage. This is
direct kernel evidence that this kill was memory exhaustion. No
jetsam reports were retained for the other five external-SIGKILL
