\title{How Far Can 16\,GB Go? Characterizing Memory-Efficient LLM Fine-Tuning\\ on Consumer Apple Silicon}
\author{Anonymous}
\date{September 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We map the fine-tuning feasibility boundary of a 16\,GiB Apple~M4 Mac mini
with a preregistered benchmark: 118 supervised runs over the Qwen3 family
(0.6B--14B), BF16 LoRA vs.\ 4-bit QLoRA, context lengths 512--8192, LoRA
ranks 4--32, and micro-batches 1--8, each with full provenance (git commit,
model revision, per-second swap sampling, SHA-256 manifests). We find:
(1) 4-bit QLoRA cuts peak training memory to 0.54--0.73$\times$ of BF16,
makes 8B the highest reproducibly trainable scale in this benchmark
(three-of-three formal seeds), and keeps step time within the
preregistered $\pm$25\% equivalence margin at all three paired scales;
(2) the transition from reproducibly trainable configurations to
system-state-dependent boundary behavior is directly observable --- the
same 4B BF16 or 14B 4-bit configuration that trains in minutes at zero
swap residency stalls at 50\% completion or makes zero progress when
several GiB of swap are resident, and we report 14B as an explicit
boundary case rather than a completed formal cell; (3) peak memory grows \emph{sub}linearly with model
size (log-log slope 0.57 for 4-bit, $R^2$=0.99) because fixed runtime
overhead dilutes weight costs, while step time grows linearly (slope
1.00); (4) context length, not model scale, is the binding constraint:
2048-token contexts complete but raise peak memory beyond physical RAM,
and 4096-token contexts are killed before their first step; (5) micro-batch
scaling fails outright at batch~8 (consistent SIGKILL with system swap
driven to $\sim$20\,GiB) and \emph{reduces} throughput at every trainable
size. All numbers are generated programmatically from immutable raw
results; seven preregistration deviations are reported in full.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Consumer Apple Silicon machines ship with a single 16\,GiB unified-memory
pool that the GPU shares with the OS. Whether, and under what conditions,
such a machine can fine-tune modern LLMs is usually answered anecdotally.
This paper answers it with a preregistered, failure-inclusive benchmark:
every configuration in a frozen matrix was run under an experiment
supervisor that recorded provenance, per-second swap sampling, and complete
logs, and every completed or failed run is retained as evidence.

Our contributions: (i) a preregistered feasibility map for Qwen3
0.6B--14B under BF16 LoRA, 4-bit QLoRA, and (exploratory) full
fine-tuning, with 8B 4-bit identified as the highest reproducibly
trainable configuration and 14B reported as an explicit
system-state-dependent boundary case; (ii) quantified memory/time
trade-offs with frozen equivalence margins; (iii) context-length and
micro-batch boundaries expressed as intervals with system-state
confounders documented; (iv) an audit showing that \trainable is a
joint property of the model and the machine's memory-residency state,
not of the model alone; and (v) a full reproducibility chain from raw
immutable results to every number in this paper, including eight
logged deviations from preregistration.

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\subsection{Apple Silicon and Unified Memory}
Apple Silicon SoCs present a single physical memory pool shared by CPU and GPU.
\texttt{mlx}~\cite{mlx2023} is an array framework that targets this architecture
directly; \texttt{mlx-lm}~\cite{mlxlm} provides LLM fine-tuning on top of it.
Prior profiling of Apple Silicon training reports substantial gaps to NVIDIA
GPUs and identifies page faults and kernel-launch overhead as system-level
contributors~\cite{feng2025profiling}. Benchmarking of MLX itself has so far
focused on inference latency for encoder models~\cite{ajayi2025benchmarking};
consumer-device LLM stacks beyond MLX include llama.cpp~\cite{llamacpp}.

\subsection{MLX}
The MLX framework exposes Metal-backed computation with lazy evaluation and a
composable optimizer API~\cite{mlx2023}. Our measurement validation
(Sec.~\ref{sec:measurements}) quantifies why explicit synchronization is
required for per-step timing under lazy execution.

\subsection{LoRA and QLoRA}
LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora} parameterizes weight updates with low-rank adapters;
QLoRA~\cite{dettmers2023qlora} backpropagates through 4-bit quantized frozen
weights, following the broader line of LLM quantization
work~\cite{dettmers2022llmint8}. Both reduce the memory footprint relative to
full fine-tuning~\cite{houlsby2019parameter,chen2016training}, and
GaLore~\cite{zhao2024galore} targets the same budget from the optimizer-state
side. These methods are largely evaluated on data-center GPUs; how they behave
inside a 16\,GB unified-memory budget is the subject of this paper.

\subsection{Resource-Constrained LLM Fine-Tuning}
Prior systems work has profiled Apple Silicon for ML training
end-to-end~\cite{feng2025profiling} and benchmarked MLX inference for encoder
models~\cite{ajayi2025benchmarking}, but the fine-tuning feasibility boundary
on a fixed consumer unified-memory budget --- which model scales, precisions,
and context lengths admit \emph{completed} training steps, and at what cost ---
has not, to our knowledge, been mapped by a preregistered benchmark. Closest
in spirit, memory-efficiency methods such as GaLore~\cite{zhao2024galore} and
QLoRA~\cite{dettmers2023qlora} report feasibility on GPU hardware where
CPU/GPU memory are separate; unified memory changes the failure mode (page
thrumbling rather than allocator exceptions), which motivates our measurement
design.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Research Questions}
We study:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RQ1} How do memory, step time, and throughput of BF16 LoRA and
        4-bit QLoRA scale with model size?
  \item \textbf{RQ2} How far does 4-bit quantization extend the
        \trainable boundary?
  \item \textbf{RQ3} Where is the context-length boundary?
  \item \textbf{RQ4} Does the memory saving of quantization come with a
        measurable training-time penalty?
  \item \textbf{RQ5} How large is the gap between ``training can start'' and
        \practical fine-tuning?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Hardware and Software}
All experiments run on a single Apple Mac mini with an Apple M4 SoC, 16\,GiB
unified memory, and macOS~26.5; the exact preflight-recorded environment is in
Table~\ref{tab:setup}. Software was locked with uv: Python~3.13.11,
\texttt{mlx}~0.32.2, \texttt{mlx-lm}~0.31.3. The M4 generation context is from
Apple's release material~\cite{applem4}.

\subsection{Models}
We use the Qwen3 family~\cite{yang2025qwen3}: instruction-tuned chat models
(not base models) at 0.6B/1.7B/4B in BF16 and 0.6B--14B as pre-quantized 4-bit
mlx-community conversions; every repository is pinned to a Hub revision and
byte-verified (Table~\ref{tab:setup}).

\subsection{Dataset}
The formal benchmark uses a frozen subset of
ultrachat\_200k~\cite{ding2023ultrachat} (revision \texttt{8049631c}): 2{,}048
training and 32 validation conversations sampled with seed 42 and formatted
with the Qwen3 chat template; loss is a standard LM loss over all tokens.
The manifest and SHA-256 sums ship with the repository. Synthetic long-context
workloads are used only for context-scaling systems analysis, never for
quality claims.

\subsection{Fine-Tuning Methods}
BF16 LoRA (rank 8, $\alpha$=16, dropout 0, targets q/k/v/o\_proj), 4-bit QLoRA
(same adapters, group size 64), and, for the smallest model only, full
fine-tuning. Optimizer AdamW, lr $10^{-4}$, constant schedule, micro-batch 1.

\subsection{Measurement Methodology}
\label{sec:measurements}
Every run is launched by an Experiment Supervisor that records git provenance,
hardware, memory/swap state (before, after, and 1\,Hz sampling), full
stdout/stderr, per-step timings, and an atomic SHA-256 manifest; failures are
first-class results. Step timing includes forward, backward, optimizer update,
and an explicit \texttt{mx.eval} synchronization; we measured that omitting
this synchronization under-reports step time by three orders of magnitude
under MLX's lazy execution. Peak memory is the MLX allocator's Metal-buffer
high-water mark (\texttt{get\_peak\_memory}); it is not process RSS, and on
unified memory it may exceed physical RAM, at which point macOS paging carries
the excess. Full validation evidence is in the repository
(\texttt{docs/measurement\_validation.md}).

\subsection{Operational Definitions}
\label{sec:definitions}
\loadable: the training process loads the model weights.
\trainable: the run completes every requested optimizer step with
\texttt{terminal\_state=success} and no kill signal.
\practical: \trainable and (P1) sampled peak system swap does not exceed the
pre-run level by more than 4\,GiB, and (P2) median step time $\le$10\,s.
\efficient: \practical with throughput $\ge$100 loss-bearing tokens/s.
All thresholds were frozen before any formal run
(research/preregistration.md); we report sensitivity to $\pm 2\times$
threshold changes. SIGKILL terminations are never reclassified as OOM without
direct kernel evidence.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
Formal configurations, the dataset freeze, the analysis pipeline, and this
paper's tables and figures are generated from raw results by committed scripts;
no number is hand-typed. Appendix~\ref{app:repro} lists reproduction commands.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Model-Scale Feasibility}
Table~\ref{tab:matrix} summarizes the preregistered matrix at ctx512.
All BF16 LoRA and 4-bit QLoRA cells through 1.7B complete their three
seeds; 4-bit completes at 4B and 8B as well (8B: three seeds, 1{,}644--1{,}694\,s
per 100-step run, 9.19\,GiB peak) --- 8B 4-bit is the highest
\emph{reproducibly trainable} configuration in this benchmark. The 14B
4-bit cell is deliberately reported as a system-state-dependent boundary
case rather than a completed or failed formal cell (deviation D8;
Sec.~\ref{sec:boundary14b}). The 4B BF16 cell shows regime dependence
(D1): it trains in 451\,s at zero residency but produced zero completed
steps in 26 minutes at 6\,GiB residency. 8B BF16 was preregistered as a
probe only and timed out; 14B BF16 was declared out-of-budget without
observation. Full fine-tuning of 0.6B completes but diverges at the
frozen lr $10^{-4}$ (final validation loss 4.44 vs.\ 1.93 for 4-bit
QLoRA) --- a negative quality result we retain as-is.

\subsection{Memory Scaling}
Figure~\ref{fig:memscale} shows peak MLX-allocator memory versus logical
parameters. Contrary to the naive linear expectation, memory grows
\emph{sub}linearly: log-log slope $0.57\pm0.05$ ($R^2$=0.99, $n$=4) for
4-bit QLoRA and $0.71\pm0.07$ ($R^2$=0.99, $n$=3) for BF16 LoRA. The
mechanism is fixed-overhead dilution: at 0.6B the 4-bit weights occupy
only $\sim$0.34\,GiB of a 2.13\,GiB peak, while at 8B the split is
4.6\,GiB weights inside a 9.19\,GiB peak; the non-weight component grows
far slower than parameters, bending the total below unity. We therefore
\emph{reject} the linear-memory hypothesis (audit H4:
unsupported) --- a practical consequence is that memory ceilings bind
later than parameter-proportional estimates suggest.

\subsection{Training-Time Scaling}
Step time scales linearly, not sublinearly, with model size
(log-log slope $1.00\pm0.09$, $R^2$=0.99, 4-bit series, $n$=4; audit H5:
partially supported, approx.\ linear). All timing figures in this
benchmark are Tier-B under our preregistered paging stratification
(D4): every formal run exchanged $>$50\,MB of swap-in per step under the
machine's multi-GiB residency baseline, so absolute step times are
descriptive, paging-confounded values --- e.g.\ 4-bit 4B at 3.66\,s/step
here versus 0.22\,s/step for the light-load probe of the same
configuration. Cross-model \emph{ratios} and paired comparisons (same
window, same residency) remain interpretable; absolute latencies from
different windows are not compared anywhere in this paper.

\subsection{Quantization Trade-offs}
Across all paired scales, 4-bit QLoRA reduces peak memory to
0.73$\times$ (0.6B), 0.59$\times$ (1.7B), and 0.54$\times$ (4B) of the
BF16 LoRA value ($n$=7 seed-pairs; audit H1: supported). Step-time
ratios are 1.10--1.14, 1.21--1.23, and 1.21 --- all inside the
preregistered $\pm$25\% equivalence margin --- and throughput ratios are
0.81--0.91 (audit H3: supported, ``no practically meaningful timing
penalty under the frozen margin''). Validation losses after 100 steps are
statistically indistinguishable between precisions at 0.6B (1.93 vs.\
1.81) and 1.7B (1.63 vs.\ 1.55).

\subsection{Batch and Rank Sensitivity}
Micro-batch scaling on 4-bit 4B (Table~\ref{tab:batch},
Figure~\ref{fig:batch}) monotonically \emph{reduces} throughput ---
89.5, 66.4, and 34.6 loss-bearing tokens/s at batch 1, 2, and 4 ---
while peak memory climbs 6.19$\rightarrow$10.04$\rightarrow$16.98\,GiB;
batch~8 is killed by the OS before completing a step in all three seeds
(exit 137, zero stdout, system swap driven to $\sim$20\,GiB). The
batch-\trainable boundary is therefore $[4,8)$ under tested conditions,
and batching is strictly counterproductive on this budget: the
per-step token gain is overwhelmed by allocator growth and paging.
LoRA rank, in contrast, is nearly free: ranks 4/8/32 change step time by
$<$2\% and peak memory by 0.24\,GiB (Table~\ref{tab:rank}).

\subsection{Context-Length Boundary}
Context length dominates every other axis (Figure~\ref{fig:ctx}): at 4B
4-bit, ctx1024 multiplies median step time by 6.6$\times$ versus ctx512
(3.66$\rightarrow$24.0\,s) and peak memory by 1.8$\times$
(6.19$\rightarrow$11.16\,GiB); ctx2048 completes all three seeds but
drives peak memory to 23.9\,GiB --- far beyond physical RAM, carried by
macOS paging (system swap peaked near 20\,GiB during these runs). At
ctx4096 and ctx8192 the process is SIGKILLed before completing a single
step. The \trainable context boundary is thus $[2048,4096)$, reported as
an interval with the residency confounder attached; within Tier-B
formal data the time amplification over ctx512 (5.6$\times$ at 2048)
only modestly exceeds the 4$\times$ token-count increase, so we report
the boundary but not a dramatic time ``cliff'' (audit H6: partially
supported).

\subsection{Training Effectiveness}
Validation loss after 100 steps is comparable across precisions
(Table~\ref{tab:matrix}) and decreases monotonically along the recorded
trajectory in every trainable run. The divergent full-FT cell (0.6B,
lr $10^{-4}$, validation loss 4.44) is retained as a negative result;
we make no instruction-following claims --- the objective is LM loss on
ultrachat\_200k with all tokens counted.

\subsection{Boundary Behavior at 14B}
\label{sec:boundary14b}
We report the 14B 4-bit cell as an explicit boundary case study (D8):
a configuration observed to enter training under favorable conditions,
but not reproducibly sustained under the formal workload.

\paragraph{Observation}
Three raw records constitute the evidence. (1) A 20-step probe at
2.7\,GiB swap residency loaded and trained at 0.68\,s/step
(\texttt{success}). (2) A formal attempt launched at 7.1\,GiB residency
completed zero steps in 24 minutes before the operator interrupted it.
(3) A second formal attempt at 3.7\,GiB residency exhibited $\sim$40
minutes of model loading, then progressed to 50 of 100 steps with step
times degrading from 27 to 86\,s (with a $>$10-minute stall at step 50)
before the 7{,}200\,s timeout killed it; its step-by-step evidence is
preserved in the raw stdout log. All three records are retained
unaltered; two further attempts were never executed (D8).

\paragraph{Interpretation}
These observations are consistent with 14B crossing from reproducible
training into a regime where system memory residency and paging state
materially affect execution --- the same regime dependence documented
for 4B BF16 (D1), reached earlier in the scale ladder. This is an
interpretation, not a direct measurement of untrainability: we did not
observe allocator exhaustion, and the probe demonstrates that favorable
conditions exist.

\paragraph{Limitation}
We did not complete a three-seed formal series at 14B, did not attempt
to manufacture a low-residency window (no reboots or system-state
manipulation to chase success, per D8), and therefore can neither claim
14B is reproducibly trainable nor that it is impossible on 16\,GiB; the
preregistered 14B ctx2048 probe was cancelled without execution. The
honest summary is: under the tested formal workload and observed system
states, 14B 4-bit QLoRA was not sustained to completion, while a
favorable-state probe did complete.

\section{Failure Analysis}
Of 118 finalized runs, 27 are failures --- all retained, none
reclassified as OOM without kernel evidence. The taxonomy
(Table~\ref{tab:failures}) splits into: OS kills under memory pressure
(batch-8 runs, ctx4096/8192 probes: exit 137 with zero or partial
output); timeouts under swap residency (14B formal, 8B BF16 probe);
operator interrupts now documented as system-state evidence (D1's 26-minute
zero-step 4B BF16 run); and nine formal attempts invalidated by a
trainer implementation bug (D5) whose variable-length validation
batching crashed for micro-batch $\ge$2 --- these are excluded from
aggregation by an error-signature rule, rerun in full on the fixed
trainer (12/12 valid outcomes), and preserved immutably. A coverage
audit reconciles all 118 raw runs against the processed table with
machine-readable dispositions (46 aggregated successes, 14 duplicates,
19 superseded, 9 implementation-invalid, 9 failed, 21 non-formal); all
46 aggregated runs pass SHA-256 manifest re-verification, while five
legacy interrupted runs lack manifests (D6, zero aggregation impact).

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{What Does 16\,GB Actually Enable?}
The results separate cleanly into three tiers. Through 4B, 4-bit QLoRA
is \trainable and \practical (median step $\le$10\,s) with stable
three-seed reproduction. At 8B, 4-bit remains \trainable across all
seeds but sits at the \practical threshold (10.0\,s/step, 42.9
tokens/s) --- reproducible, not \efficient. At 14B, behavior becomes
system-state-dependent: favorable conditions admit real training
steps, while the formal workload was not sustained under observed
residencies. BF16 LoRA is \practical through 1.7B. The binding
constraints are, in order, context length, micro-batch, then model
scale --- inverting the usual intuition that parameters come first.
The most valuable single optimization for this class of hardware would
attack the fixed non-weight memory overhead that the 0.57 memory slope
implicates.

\subsection{Loadable vs Trainable vs Practical}
The three-layer vocabulary is not academic: every ``boundary'' in this
benchmark moved with system state. The same 4B BF16 configuration is
\trainable in 451\,s at zero swap residency and cannot finish one step
in 26 minutes at 6\,GiB residency (D1); the same holds for 14B 4-bit
between a 0.68\,s/step probe and a 50/100-step timeout (D7). Feasibility
claims that do not report swap residency are therefore not reproducible
in kind. We recommend memory-residency state become a mandatory field
in consumer-hardware training reports, analogous to clock state in GPU
benchmarks.

\subsection{Implications for Consumer LLM Research}
Three practical rules follow. (1) Quantization pays twice on unified
memory: it buys headroom (0.54--0.73$\times$ memory) at effectively no
time cost within a wide margin, and extends reach by at least two model
sizes. (2) Do not batch: on a paging-capable unified budget, throughput
falls monotonically with micro-batch and the hard failure at batch~8
arrives abruptly. (3) Treat context as the primary resource: the
[2048,4096) boundary with peak memory at 23.9\,GiB (vs.\ 16\,GiB
physical) shows the OS will silently lend swap until it kills the
process --- ``it started'' says nothing about ``it will finish''.

\subsection{Limitations}
Single machine, single SoC generation (M4, 16\,GiB); absolute times are
Tier-B and window-specific (D4) --- we compare only within-window
ratios; LM-loss-only objectives with no prompt masking; 20-step runs on
the context/batch/rank axes limit distributional detail (60 measured
steps per cell after warm-up exclusion); system-level swap counters
include background processes; and the 14B cell is reported as a
boundary case study rather than a completed three-seed formal result
--- we stopped additional 14B attempts as a declared decision (D8)
rather than manufacturing favorable system states, so 14B is neither
claimed reproducible nor impossible. Deviations D1--D8 are reported in
full in the repository.

\subsection{Ethics and Licensing}
The benchmark targets publicly released model weights (Apache~2.0 for Qwen3)
and a publicly released dataset (ultrachat\_200k, research-permitted license);
no synthetic workload contributes to quality claims. All measurements were
performed on the authors' own hardware.

\section{Conclusion}
On a 16\,GiB unified-memory Mac, 4-bit QLoRA is the default answer for
fine-tuning: it roughly halves memory at no measurable time cost
(frozen $\pm$25\% margin), trains 8B-scale models to completion, and
leaves rank choice nearly free. The boundaries that actually bind are
context ($\in[2048,4096)$ tokens) and micro-batch ($\in[4,8)$, with
monotonically negative throughput returns), and both --- like the 4B
BF16 and 14B cells --- shift with the machine's swap residency,
making system-state reporting a prerequisite for any reproducible
consumer-hardware training claim. Every number above is regenerated
from immutable raw runs by committed scripts; the failures are part of
the result.

\section{Measurement Validation}
\label{app:measurements}
Summary of \texttt{docs/measurement\_validation.md}: (A) step timing requires
the explicit \texttt{mx.eval} synchronization present in the trainer;
asynchronous dispatch under-reports step time by $\sim$4{,}500$\times$ on a
reference workload. (B) \texttt{get\_peak\_memory} counts MLX allocator
buffers in bytes; a 1\,GiB allocation increases both active and peak memory by
exactly 1\,GiB after reset. (C) All raw results are arithmetically consistent
with their per-step timing artifacts under their declared interval
definitions. (D) The 1\,Hz swap sampler consumes 1.47\% of one CPU core at
5$\times$ the formal cadence.
